Christian Mission

Understanding the Great Commission

Exploring the biblical command of Jesus Christ to make disciples, teach the nations, and participate faithfully in Christian mission.

Global College of Missiology   •   Christian Mission

The Great Commission is one of the most recognized passages concerning Christian mission. Jesus Christ entrusted His followers with the responsibility of making disciples and teaching them to observe His commands.

The Great Commission is therefore more than a statement about travelling to distant countries. It presents a broad calling to participate in the work of making disciples and communicating the Christian message.

What Is the Great Commission?

The expression Great Commission is commonly used to describe Jesus' instructions to His disciples following His resurrection, particularly the command recorded in Matthew 28:18–20.

"Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you." Matthew 28:19–20

The passage connects several important aspects of Christian mission: going, making disciples, baptizing, and teaching.

The Authority Behind the Commission

Before giving the command to His disciples, Jesus declares that all authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Him.

This statement provides the foundation for the command that follows. Christian mission is presented as a response to the authority of Christ rather than simply a human programme or institutional activity.

Mission Begins With Christ

The Great Commission is rooted in the person and authority of Jesus Christ. Christian mission is therefore connected to His teaching, His example, and His command to make disciples.

"Go" and the Call to Mission

The Great Commission contains a movement toward people and communities. The disciples were not instructed simply to remain where they were and wait for people to come to them.

Christian mission can therefore involve crossing geographical, cultural, linguistic, and social boundaries in order to communicate the Gospel and serve people.

For some people this may mean serving in another country. For others it may mean serving faithfully within their own community, church, workplace, neighbourhood, or social environment.

Making Disciples

One of the central ideas in the Great Commission is discipleship.

The command is not limited to encouraging people to make an initial profession of faith. Jesus speaks about making disciples and teaching them to observe His commands.

Discipleship therefore involves an ongoing process of learning, spiritual growth, obedience, and participation in the life of faith.

This means that Christian mission includes both evangelism and continued spiritual formation.

Baptism and Christian Identity

Jesus also includes baptism within the Great Commission.

Baptism is presented as an important part of the discipleship process and is associated with the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

This highlights that Christian mission involves bringing people into the life and community of Christian faith rather than treating conversion as an isolated event.

Teaching the Commands of Christ

The Great Commission concludes with the responsibility to teach disciples to observe everything Jesus has commanded.

Teaching is therefore an important part of Christian mission.

Mission workers, pastors, teachers, and church leaders have the responsibility of helping believers understand Scripture and grow in their knowledge and practice of the Christian faith.

  • Biblical teaching
  • Discipleship
  • Spiritual formation
  • Christian leadership
  • Practical obedience

The Great Commission and All Nations

Jesus specifically speaks of "all nations." This gives the Great Commission a global dimension.

Christian mission therefore reaches beyond one language, culture, ethnicity, or geographical region.

The global nature of the command is one reason why cross-cultural understanding is important in missiology and Christian ministry.

Mission workers often encounter people whose languages, traditions, worldviews, and social structures differ significantly from their own.

The Promise of Christ's Presence

The Great Commission does not end with the command to make disciples. Jesus also gives His followers a promise of His continuing presence.

"And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age." Matthew 28:20

This promise is an important part of the passage. Christian mission is not presented as a task that believers must accomplish through human strength alone.

The Great Commission in the Early Church

The book of Acts provides examples of the early Christian community carrying the message of Christ beyond Jerusalem and into other regions.

Acts 1:8 records Jesus telling His disciples that they would be His witnesses in Jerusalem, throughout Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.

"You will be my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth." Acts 1:8

The passage illustrates the expanding geographical scope of Christian witness and provides an important biblical reference for understanding Christian mission.

What Does the Great Commission Mean Today?

The Great Commission continues to shape Christian approaches to evangelism, discipleship, church planting, theological education, leadership, and cross-cultural ministry.

Christians may participate in this calling in different ways depending on their gifts, opportunities, responsibilities, and ministry context.

Some may serve as missionaries. Others may teach, pastor churches, mentor believers, support mission organizations, train leaders, plant churches, or faithfully share their faith within their communities.

Preparation for Great Commission Ministry

Effective mission requires more than enthusiasm. Those preparing for ministry benefit from developing a strong understanding of Scripture, Christian theology, culture, communication, leadership, and practical ministry.

This is one reason theological and missiological education can be valuable for people preparing to serve in Christian mission.

Training can help students think carefully about the biblical message, understand the people they serve, and approach ministry with humility and responsibility.

Conclusion

The Great Commission is a central biblical foundation for Christian mission. It calls followers of Jesus Christ to make disciples, baptize, teach, and participate in the proclamation of the Christian message among all nations.

Its emphasis on discipleship also reminds the Church that mission is not only about reaching people but also about helping them grow as followers of Christ.

Understanding the Great Commission can therefore help Christians approach mission with a clearer biblical foundation and a deeper sense of responsibility toward the people and communities they are called to serve.
