Church & Mission

The Church and the Mission of God

The Church is called to participate in God's mission through worship, witness, discipleship, fellowship, service, and the proclamation of the Gospel among all peoples.

Global College of Missiology • Church & Mission

The mission of the Church is closely connected to the mission of God. Throughout Scripture, God is revealed as actively working to redeem, restore, and draw people to Himself.

The Church does not exist only as a gathering for worship. The people of God are also called to live as witnesses to Christ, make disciples, serve others, and communicate the Gospel.

Understanding this relationship between the Church and God's mission helps Christians see mission not as an activity reserved for a small group of specialists, but as an important dimension of the Church's calling.

What Is the Mission of God?

The phrase “mission of God” is often used to describe God's work and purpose in the world. Christian mission begins with God rather than with human initiative.

Scripture presents God as the Creator and Redeemer who calls people to Himself and works toward the fulfillment of His purposes.

The Church participates in this mission through its witness, worship, discipleship, teaching, service, and proclamation of the Gospel.

“For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son.”

John 3:16

The Church Belongs to God

The Church is not ultimately a human organization. It is the community of people who have been called to follow Jesus Christ.

This identity matters for mission because the Church's purpose should be shaped by God rather than simply by organizational preferences.

Churches may have different structures, traditions, programs, and ministry approaches, but their identity as followers of Christ gives them a common foundation for mission.

The Great Commission

One of the clearest passages concerning the Church's mission is the Great Commission given by Jesus after His resurrection.

“Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.”

Matthew 28:19

The command includes going, making disciples, baptizing, and teaching. Mission is therefore closely connected with discipleship rather than being limited to an initial proclamation of faith.

The Church as a Witness

Jesus told His followers that they would be His witnesses. This witness involves communicating what Christians believe about Christ and living in ways that reflect the values of the Gospel.

Christian witness can take place through preaching, personal conversations, teaching, relationships, acts of service, and the life of the Christian community.

The Church's public witness is strengthened when its words and actions are consistent with the message it proclaims.

Worship and Mission

Worship and mission are closely connected. The Church worships God because of who He is and what He has done.

Genuine worship also shapes the priorities of believers. As Christians understand God's character and purposes, they are encouraged to live in obedience and participate in His work.

Mission should therefore not be separated completely from the worshiping life of the Church.

A Helpful Perspective

The Church does not gather only to prepare for mission and then stop being missional when it leaves the building. Christian identity and witness should shape everyday life.

Discipleship Is Central to Mission

Jesus' command to make disciples demonstrates that Christian mission includes helping people grow in their relationship with Him.

Discipleship involves teaching, encouragement, spiritual formation, accountability, fellowship, prayer, and practical obedience.

Churches can support discipleship through Bible studies, small groups, mentoring, preaching, teaching, youth ministry, leadership development, and personal relationships.

The Church and the Nations

The Great Commission extends the Church's vision beyond its immediate community. Jesus spoke of making disciples among all nations.

This gives the Church a global perspective. Christians are called to recognize that God's concern extends across cultures, languages, peoples, and geographical boundaries.

Some believers may serve internationally, while others may participate in mission within their own communities. Both local and cross-cultural ministry can be expressions of Christian witness.

Local Mission Matters

Global mission should not cause Christians to overlook the people around them.

Families, neighborhoods, schools, workplaces, cities, and local communities provide opportunities for Christian witness and service.

A church can participate in mission through hospitality, evangelism, discipleship, community service, prayer, counseling, teaching, youth ministry, and practical support for people in need.

The Church as a Community

Mission is not only an individual responsibility. The Church is a community, and Christian witness is strengthened when believers serve together.

Different members of the Church have different gifts, experiences, and responsibilities. These differences can contribute to a broader ministry when people work together.

“Now you are the body of Christ, and each one of you is a part of it.”

1 Corinthians 12:27

Every Believer Has a Role

Christian mission should not be viewed as the responsibility of pastors, missionaries, or ministry leaders alone.

Believers can participate through prayer, giving, hospitality, evangelism, discipleship, teaching, administration, encouragement, service, and many other forms of ministry.

The Church becomes stronger when believers understand that their gifts and opportunities can contribute to the wider mission.

Mission and Service

Christian mission includes serving people. Jesus repeatedly demonstrated concern for people who were hurting, excluded, hungry, sick, or in need.

Service does not replace the proclamation of the Gospel, nor should proclamation be used as an excuse to ignore practical needs.

Christian communities can demonstrate God's love through compassionate and responsible service while remaining clear about their faith.

Cross-Cultural Mission

The global nature of the Church means Christians may serve people whose languages, traditions, histories, and social structures differ from their own.

Cross-cultural mission requires humility and willingness to learn. Mission workers should listen to local believers, understand cultural contexts, learn appropriate forms of communication, and avoid assuming that their own culture is the standard for Christian faithfulness.

The Gospel can be communicated across cultures while recognizing legitimate cultural differences.

The Role of Local Churches in Mission

Local churches can play an important role in preparing and supporting people for mission.

This may include identifying gifts, providing discipleship, offering ministry opportunities, encouraging spiritual development, supporting mission workers, and praying for Christian ministry around the world.

Churches can also help future mission workers understand that preparation involves both spiritual formation and practical training.

Training the Church for Mission

Churches need people who understand Scripture, communicate clearly, work with others, navigate cultural differences, and develop disciples.

Missionary and ministry training can help equip believers for these responsibilities.

Training may include biblical studies, missiology, theology, Christian leadership, discipleship, communication, cross-cultural ministry, and practical ministry experience.

Equipping for Mission

A Church that equips its members for service creates more opportunities for believers to participate actively in the mission of God.

Prayer and the Mission of God

Prayer is an important part of Christian mission. Churches can pray for missionaries, local ministries, unreached communities, leaders, new believers, families, and opportunities to communicate the Gospel.

Prayer also reminds Christians that mission ultimately depends on God rather than human strength alone.

“Ask the Lord of the harvest, therefore, to send out workers into his harvest field.”

Matthew 9:38

Mission and Leadership

Healthy mission requires leaders who can equip people, organize ministry, develop teams, resolve conflicts, and encourage spiritual growth.

Christian leadership should follow the example of Jesus, emphasizing service rather than personal status.

Leaders can help the Church remain focused on its mission by continually reminding believers of their identity, purpose, responsibilities, and opportunities for service.

Mission and Unity

The Christian Church includes people from many cultures, languages, backgrounds, and traditions.

Mission provides opportunities for Christians to demonstrate unity around their shared faith in Christ while learning to work through legitimate differences.

Cooperation can strengthen Christian witness and create opportunities for ministries to serve communities more effectively.

The Church's Mission in Everyday Life

Mission is not limited to special events, conferences, trips, or organized outreach programs.

Christians can live missionally through ordinary relationships and responsibilities.

  • A parent can disciple children.
  • A teacher can demonstrate integrity and compassion.
  • A business owner can practice honesty and fairness.
  • A student can develop relationships and communicate Christian faith respectfully.
  • A church member can welcome and encourage others.
  • A believer can pray for people and serve their community.

Everyday faithfulness can become an important expression of Christian witness.

The Church and the Future of Mission

The mission of the Church continues from one generation to another. This makes discipleship and leadership development especially important.

Churches can invest in younger believers, train future leaders, encourage missionary service, and create opportunities for people to discover and develop their ministry gifts.

Preparing the next generation helps the Church continue its witness and service in changing cultural contexts.

A Church Prepared for Mission

Characteristics of a Mission-Minded Church
  • It understands its identity in Christ.
  • It teaches Scripture and develops disciples.
  • It encourages prayer for God's mission.
  • It equips believers for ministry and service.
  • It welcomes opportunities for local and cross-cultural witness.
  • It values teamwork and healthy leadership.
  • It serves people with compassion and integrity.
  • It develops future leaders and ministry workers.
  • It remains committed to learning and spiritual growth.

Conclusion

The Church is called to participate in the mission of God. This mission includes worship, witness, discipleship, fellowship, service, leadership, and the proclamation of the Gospel.

The Great Commission gives the Church a global vision, while everyday Christian life provides countless opportunities for faithful witness within local communities.

Mission is therefore not simply an activity performed by a small group of people. The entire Church can participate through the gifts, opportunities, relationships, and responsibilities God has given its members.

A Church that understands its identity and calling can equip believers to serve faithfully, develop disciples, reach across cultures, and participate in God's work in the world.
